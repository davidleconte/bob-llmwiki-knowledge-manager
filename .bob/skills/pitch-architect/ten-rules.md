# The Ten Rules

Apply these to everything produced with this skill.

1. **Establish "Why Change" before the capability case.** Earn the right to present your value; never open with your solution.

2. **Client is the hero — say "you," never "we,"** even when describing your own capabilities.

3. **Open on the audience's goal, then the change that threatens it.**

4. **Pain before gain.** Make the risk of no change concrete *before* the upside (a loss lands ~2–3× harder than an equivalent gain).

5. **Land an unconsidered need** — reframe a problem the audience hadn't connected; don't recite data they already have.

6. **Own the value wedge:** the capability must be *relevant* to the audience, *unique* to the user, and *provable*. Flag anything that's value parity (competitors can claim it too). Never call a capability uniquely yours unless it's true and defendable.

7. **Convey every point — especially the value edge — as Means → Do → Is.** Lead with what it *means* to them (tied to a KPI), then what they can *do*, then the underlying capability (*is*) last, as proof.

8. **Three core points maximum.** People hold ~3; a 4th is forgotten, a 5th confuses.

9. **Tie every claim to a KPI the audience personally owns.** Match the message to their level (don't pitch cost cuts to someone who hears "am I getting cut?").

10. **Visuals carry the story; stay empathetic, never fearful.** One idea per visual. Raise the stakes honestly, then always offer a way forward.
