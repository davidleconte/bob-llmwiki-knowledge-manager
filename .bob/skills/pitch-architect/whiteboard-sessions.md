# Whiteboard Session Pattern

Use this pattern when the deliverable is a live whiteboard talk (typically 10–20 minutes) rather than a slide deck. The output is a **facilitator guide**: the finished board first, then illustrated build steps.

## Board design

- **Three zones, left to right, drawn in order:** (1) the wake-up call — a stat-led hook; (2) a plain-language tutorial that builds shared understanding and ends in the pain; (3) the better way — capabilities, outcomes, and the reveal.
- **The finished board must tell the whole story on its own** — someone walking in at the end should be able to read it. That's the test of the layout.
- **Marker color code:** black = facts and tutorial, red = pain, blue = solution/capabilities, green = outcomes. The switch to blue visibly signals "new chapter."
- **One idea per drawing.** Simple icons (cylinders, arrows, brick walls, stick figures, clouds) — sketch quality is a feature; it keeps attention on the story.
- **Solid vs. dashed lines carry meaning:** solid arrows = data/things that move; dashed threads = connection without movement.
- **Leave the reveal for last:** reserve a blank spot (bottom right) where the partner name lands in the final two minutes — after the room asks "so how would you make that possible?"

## Facilitator guide format

1. **Title + how-the-session-works page:** the arc with per-step timing, ground rules (say "you," no products, don't name the partner until the reveal), and materials (markers by color, board space).
2. **The finished board** — full view, labeled "what the room sees at minute N."
3. **One page per build step** showing the board as it accumulates: new strokes in full color, earlier work faded. Each page has a **YOU DRAW** panel (what to add, which marker) and a **YOU SAY** panel (tight spoken beats), with the full narration script in speaker notes.
4. **Land-the-close page:** a small, specific, low-risk ask; KPI tie-backs; next steps (photograph the board — hand-drawn boards get forwarded internally more than decks).
5. **"If they ask…" page:** likely objections with capability-level answers.
6. **Stats & sources page** (presenter-only): the fuller numbers behind each hook stat, with the reminder that they're attention-getters, not audit numbers.

## Session mechanics

- Hook stats: three maximum, each written big and circled in red as it's spoken. End the hook with an engagement question; remember the answer and hand it back during the pain step.
- Ask one question per zone; audience answers become ammunition for the pain step. If someone names something you haven't drawn, add it in their words — it becomes their board.
- Build the pain fully before offering the way out; let the discomfort sit for one beat.
- After the capabilities are drawn, stop and wait. The room asking "so how would you make that possible?" is the cue for the reveal — that question means the session worked.
- Coach "know your room" adaptations in the notes (e.g., relabeling a drawn element to match the client's actual environment live).
